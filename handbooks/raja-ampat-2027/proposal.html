01 — Why now

You've seen the photo. This is the trip.

Everyone knows the Piaynemo shot. Very few people have stood at the top of those steps early, before the boats arrive, with the lagoons laid out below like someone spilled the sea in two shades of blue.

Raja Ampat sits at the top of the Coral Triangle — more species of coral and reef fish than anywhere else on earth. It's remote enough that it still feels like a discovery — and from Bali, it's one domestic route away.

February is calm-water season. Clear visibility, easy crossings, long light. We've built five days around the four places that matter most: Piaynemo, Batanta, Kabui and Kalibiru.

Some places are meant to be seen. Raja Ampat is meant to be in.

03 — The days

Five days, four islands

  1. Day 121 Feb

    Arrival

    Sorong → Raja Ampat

    Bali to Sorong, then across the water into the islands. The rest of the afternoon is yours — the first sunset sets the tone for everything after.

  2. Day 222 Feb

    Piaynemo

    Piaynemo · Arborek · Pasir Timbul

    The climb to the Piaynemo lookout: the karst view everyone has seen, now without a screen between you and it. Then Arborek, a village that lives on and with the reef, and Pasir Timbul — a sandbar that exists only when the tide allows.

  3. Day 323 Feb

    The reefs

    Waringkabom · Sawandarek · Yenbuba · Friwen Monda

    A day in the water. Jetty reefs and channel drop-offs, back to back, at the heart of the most biodiverse marine region on the planet. Bring the underwater camera.

  4. Day 424 Feb

    Kabui

    Kabui Bay · Marindal Hill · Mayalibit · Kalibiru

    Narrow passages between limestone walls through Kabui Bay, a trek up Marindal Hill, the still water of Mayalibit, and Kalibiru to close out the last full day.

  5. Day 525 Feb

    Departure

    Raja Ampat → Sorong → Bali

    One last swim if the morning allows, then back to Sorong for the flight home to Bali.
