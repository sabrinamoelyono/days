Quick tips

Before you land.

Everything you need to know before you touch down in Barcelona.

TIME

Barcelona eats late

Lunch is 2–4pm. Dinner kicks off at 9pm. Don't be the table asking for dinner at 7.

BOOK

Book or queue

For Paradiso, Sips, Disfrutar and El Xampanyet — either book weeks in advance or arrive when they open.

FEST

Festival hours

Primavera runs afternoon through 3am. Pace yourself. The best sets are always after midnight.

MOVE

Getting around

Metro until midnight weekdays, 2am Fri, all night Sat. After that: Uber or taxi. Poblenou to El Born is 30 minutes.

CASH

Cash is still king

Smaller tapas bars and old-school spots often prefer cash. Keep €50–€100 on you at all times.

WEAR

Dress for two climates

Barcelona in June: warm sunny days (24–28°C), balmy evenings. Light layers for the coastal breeze at the beach stage.

The city

Barcelona, decoded.

Barcelona rewards the curious and punishes the timid. It's a city of neighbourhoods that feel like separate cities — each with its own pace, its own regulars, its own rules.

El Poblenou

Where the festival lives

The old industrial district that grew into a creative hub. Parc del Fòrum is here. The Rambla del Poblenou is the neighbourhood's spine — cafés, bars, easy energy. Henry's Bar and el 58 are both here.

El Born

Cocktails & cava

Medieval streets, the Picasso Museum, and Barcelona's best bar scene. Paradiso, El Xampanyet, Cal Pep, Bar del Pla. El Born is where most nights end up.

Barceloneta

Seafood & beach

The old fishing neighbourhood between the city and the sea. Back streets hold old-school tapas bars and fish that was in the water this morning. La Cova Fumada and Jai Ca are here.

Eixample

Gaudí & great bars

The city's orderly grid neighbourhood. Casa Batlló and La Sagrada Família are here. So are Sips, Dry Martini, Bandini's, Disfrutar, Mont Bar, and Gresca.

Poble Sec

The local secret

Where Barcelona that hasn't been gentrified yet survives. Quimet y Quimet and Xemei are here. The bar stools are small, the montaditos are brilliant.

Sant Antoni

The creative quarter

A neighbourhood in transition — in the best way. Coffee shops, natural wine bars, and chef-driven bistros have clustered around the Sant Antoni market.

Gràcia

Village within the city

Narrow streets, bohemian bars, square culture. Fonda Pepa and Brugarol are here. Great for aperitivo hour before heading further into the city.

Restaurants

Where to eat.

Twelve picks across every neighbourhood. Two are certified Dua Lipa favourites. All are worth your time.

12 picks · All neighbourhoods

DL

Dua Lipa's Barcelona favourites

Gresca and Xemei — she comes back every visit. Book these two before anything else.

01Dua Lipa

Gresca

Eixample · Modern Catalan

Dua Lipa's most-visited Barcelona restaurant

Chef Rafa Peña — winner of the Premi Nacional de Gastronomia 2023 — runs this intimate Eixample spot with an open kitchen and a bar counter where you watch everything happen. Marinated anchovies, scrambled eggs with wild garlic and prawns, octopus carpaccio, and a torrija dessert that's become mythic. Dua Lipa has been back multiple times, sitting at the bar while the chef explains every dish.

02Dua Lipa

Xemei

Poble Sec · Venetian

Dua Lipa's other Barcelona obsession

Venetian twins Stefano and Max Colombo brought the real cooking of Venice to Barcelona — sarde in saor, baccalà mantecato, and grilled baby squid with polenta. Dua Lipa has returned here on multiple visits for the pasta and the tiramisu. Considered the best Italian restaurant in the city. Organic wine list, cozy terrace on Passeig de l'Exposició.

03Splurge

Disfrutar

Eixample · Avant-garde Catalan

Barcelona's most creative kitchen, full stop

Three-Michelin-star restaurant run by alumni of elBulli. Each dish is a concept — from the signature caviar-filled panchino to the liquid salad. Playful yet precise, balancing technique and flavour in a way that feels inventive but genuinely satisfying. Book months ahead.

04Must try

Maleducat

Eixample · Modern Catalan

A bold, no-rules kind of place

Seasonal Catalan dishes with punchy flavours, a bit unexpected, and the kind of spot you leave thinking about what you just ate. The cooking is confident without being showy — proper ingredients, properly treated.

05Seafood

Besta

El Born · Seafood

Elevated yet effortless

A seafood-focused restaurant where Galician and Catalan influences come together in a way that's refined but still warm and inviting. The arroz negro and the daily fish are the reasons you come. The reason you return is harder to name.

06Chef's pick

Brugarol

Gràcia · Fire & produce

Everything feels intentional

A beautifully designed space where produce-driven plates meet open-fire cooking that brings out deep, honest flavours. The scallop tartare with cured egg yolk is the kind of dish that redefines a single ingredient. Book ahead.

07Local pick

La Bonaigua

Eixample · Steakhouse

The steak speaks for itself

Perfectly matured beef, simply executed, all about the quality of the product rather than anything overcomplicated. The kind of place carnivores bookmark and return to every trip. Go hungry.

08Casual

Casa Amalia

Gràcia · Seafood & paella

Market-style Barcelona energy

Seafood-led dishes served with the kind of comforting, no-fuss warmth that makes you feel like a local. The real standout is the paella — rich, deeply flavourful, and the kind you'd come back for without overthinking the rest of the menu.

09Classic

Cañete

Raval · Tapas counter

Best experienced at the counter

Where everything moves fast and feels alive — the grilled carabineros are a standout, sweet and smoky with just enough char, alongside silky seafood dishes that keep it classic but never boring.

10Essential

Estimar

El Born · Elevated seafood

Where El Bulli lives on — in a marisquería

Head chef Rafa Zafra is an El Bulli graduate who transformed the traditional seafood restaurant into something refined and market-fresh. Galician razor clams, Cantabrian anchovies, the famous onion & crayfish carpaccio. Book weeks ahead.

11Must try

Mont Bar

Eixample · Modern tapas

Where the food critics eat on their nights off

A fresh take on fine dining without the ceremony. Mont Bar's small plates rotate with the seasons. Intimate, excellent wine list, great energy at the bar.

12Local pick

Franca

Eixample Dreta · Modern Catalan

Local and very current at the same time

Catalan dishes reworked in thrilling modern ways. The space is welcoming, the cooking always surprises in a good way. You never know what the dish will look like when it arrives. Roger de Llúria 46.

Tapas bars

The real Barcelona.

The tapas bar is where Barcelona actually lives. Stand at the bar. Order two or three things. Move on. That's how you do it.

12 picks · From legendary to local

01Iconic

El Xampanyet

El Born

OrderHouse cava + anchovies

The original old-school cava bar, pouring since 1929. Always packed, always worth it. Order the house cava and whatever's on the counter. This is Barcelona in a glass.

02Legendary

Quimet y Quimet

Poble Sec

OrderSalmon montadito with honey

Standing room only, tins of conservas stacked floor to ceiling, brilliant montaditos. The salmon tapa with honey is the one. Go early — they close when they run out.

03Original

La Cova Fumada

Barceloneta

OrderThe original bomba

Dating back to 1944. No sign on the door, no menu — just a blackboard. This is where the bomba was invented. Worth every bit of the effort to find it.

04Seafood

Cal Pep

El Born

OrderWhatever Pep is cooking that day

The legendary seafood counter where the bar seats are the best in the house and the kitchen decides what's good that day. Trust the process.

05Festival zone

el 58

Poblenou

OrderRazor clams, duck magret, pulpo

Right on the Rambla del Poblenou — walking distance from the festival. Menu on a blackboard, terrace out back. No reservations, so show up or miss out.

06Crowd pleaser

Cerveceria Catalana

Eixample

OrderMontaditos, grilled prawns

The most-reviewed tapas bar in Barcelona. Long bar, wide selection, consistent quality. Fast service. No reservations — queue at peak hours but it moves quickly.

Bar del Pla

El Born

OrderNatural wine + market special

Over 30 natural wines, brilliant tapas, and sommeliers who know their stuff. The go-to before a night in El Born. Sits just metres from El Xampanyet.

08Historic

Bodega La Puntual

El Born

OrderArroz de Pluma Ibérica

In an 1872 coffee roaster building on Carrer de Montcada. A casual oyster bar at the entrance; a larger room for groups further inside.

Paco Meralgo

OrderOndarroa anchovies, Cíes clams

The seafood-tapas institution of the Eixample. Marinated anchovies from Ondarroa and razor clams from the Cíes Islands are the headliners.

10Old school

Jai Ca

Barceloneta

OrderBombas, bravas, cod fritters

Classic tapas bar in the old fishing district. Earthy, no-nonsense. The bombas here are the standard all others are judged against.

11All day

Bar Bocata

Sarrià-Sant Gervasi

OrderTortilla with caramelised onion

From breakfast through late dinner, Bar Bocata delivers all day. The tortilla with soft caramelised onion is non-negotiable. Book ahead.

Bar Cañete

Raval

Near the Gran Teatre del Liceu. Family-run kitchen using fish fresh from Catalan markets. Beautifully executed classics, lively room.

Bars & drinks

Stay out late.

Barcelona's bar scene is world-class. The cocktail movement here is technical and serious, the natural wine scene is booming, the vermouth culture is real, and the old cava bars have been doing their thing for decades.

The festival

Primavera Sound.

Parc del Fòrum is a concrete plaza jutting into the Mediterranean. At night, with the stages lit up and the sea behind everything, it's one of the most striking festival settings in the world.

IN

Arrival

Gates open from 4pm. The early sets are worth catching — smaller stages, fewer people, musicians who are genuinely happy to be there.

EAT

Eating & drinking

The food at Primavera is actually good. Pro move: eat a proper dinner before 8pm and graze inside.

STAGE

The main stage

The main stage faces the sea. For big acts, get there 30 minutes early or accept a great view from further back. Both are valid.

SET

Stage logistics

Multiple stages run simultaneously. Download the app, build your schedule, but leave gaps — the best moments are often accidental.

AM

The late hours

11pm to 3am is when Primavera turns into something else entirely. The sets get longer, and the festival earns its reputation.

KIT

What to wear

Comfortable shoes you won't regret at 2am. A light jacket for the sea breeze. One bag with a secure pocket for your phone and wallet.

Logistics

On the ground.

Airport, metro, money and data — the practical bit, so the rest can be anything but.

From the airport

Barcelona El Prat (BCN) is 30–40 minutes from the city centre. The Aerobus runs to Plaça Catalunya every 10 minutes. Taxi is a fixed fare (~€35). Uber also works.

Getting around

The metro covers most of the city. T-Casual 10-trip card is the best value. Poblenou/El Fòrum is on the L4 line (yellow). Metro runs all night on Saturdays.

Festival transport

Parc del Fòrum is right at El Maresme | Fòrum station on the L4. Walking from El Born takes about 30 minutes along the waterfront.

Money

Spain is largely card-friendly, but old-school tapas bars prefer cash. Revolut or Wise cards are ideal — no foreign transaction fees. Keep €50–€100 in your wallet.

Phone & data

Indonesian SIMs won't have EU roaming by default. Pick up a local SIM at the airport (Vodafone or Orange), or check your provider before you leave.
