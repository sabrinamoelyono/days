# DAYS — Brand Identity

DAYS is a culture-driven travel concierge for people who travel *because of* something: a festival lineup, a race weekend, a moment they don't want to miss. This document is the reference for how the brand looks, sounds and is named. For the legal and corporate profile of the company behind it, see [README.md](README.md).

## Positioning

A culture-driven travel concierge shaping how you move through destinations, from festivals to local scenes.

**Audience:** 25–45, Jakarta-based young professionals to established career stage, disposable income, culturally fluent. Music and culture obsessives who know the difference between going to a festival and *doing* a festival. Affluent but not flashy — they want curation, not price tags. They've outgrown group tour culture and want something that feels theirs.

**What moves them:** exclusivity without elitism, being early and in-the-know, the story they'll tell afterward, seamless logistics so they can be fully present, feeling like they belong in the room — even if the room is a muddy field in Somerset.

**What we offer:**
- Music festivals in Europe — Glastonbury, Primavera Sound, Dekmantel, Melt!, Roskilde
- Motorsport and major sport — F1 Grand Prix weekends, the Australian Open, Wimbledon, Six Nations
- Adventure and nature — *Into The Wild: Borneo*, Patagonia
- Culture and city trips — Art Basel, Milan Design Week

Every package is named and curated, not sold off a shelf.

**DAYS is not:** a group tour operator, a budget travel hack, an exclusionary luxury brand, generic ("unforgettable journeys for the discerning traveler"), or a festival ticket reseller.

## Logo

| Asset | Use |
|---|---|
| `assets/days-wordmark.png` | Primary lock-up, trimmed to the wordmark, transparent background. Use on light or dark surfaces. |
| `assets/days-logo-square.png` | Wordmark on a 500 × 500 transparent square. Use for avatars, favicons and social profile images. |

**Clear space:** keep clear space around the wordmark at least equal to the height of one letter.

**Minimum size:** don't run the wordmark narrower than ~80px wide — the rounded terminals on the D, A and S start to close up below that.

**Don't:**
- Don't recolor the wordmark outside the palette below.
- Don't add a stroke, shadow, gradient or outline.
- Don't stretch, skew or rotate it.
- Don't place it on a background that drops its contrast (busy photography, low-contrast tints).

## Colour

| Token | Hex | Use | Contrast |
|---|---|---|---|
| DAYS Orange | `#EC5B39` | Primary brand colour — logo, accents, highlights | 3.4:1 on white/paper. Large text and graphics only; not for small body text. |
| Brand Ink | `#C9431F` | Orange for text — links, emphasis on light backgrounds | 4.6–4.9:1 on white/paper. Passes for body text. |
| Ink | `#1B1714` | Primary text, dark surfaces | 16.8:1 on paper |
| Paper | `#FBF8F5` | Primary light background | — |
| Muted | `#6E655E` | Secondary text, captions | 5.4:1 on paper |
| Line | `#E8E0D8` | Borders, dividers | — |
| Tint | `#FDEEE9` | Light orange fill, for badges and highlight blocks | — |

Dark mode inverts Ink and Paper and lifts the orange slightly (`#F26A49` / `#FF8A6B`) to hold contrast on dark surfaces.

**Rule of thumb:** DAYS Orange is a brand colour, not a text colour — use it for the logo, icons, dividers, large numerals and accents. For orange text (links, labels), use Brand Ink.

## Type

- **Display / body:** Inter Tight (400 body, 500 medium, 600 semibold, 800 for headlines)
- **Data / numbers / references:** JetBrains Mono, for registration numbers, decree references, dates and figures — anywhere precision matters more than warmth

Headlines are set tight (letter-spacing −0.01 to −0.02em) and bold. Body copy stays at comfortable line height (1.5–1.6) — the brand is confident, not loud.

## Voice

**Personality:**

| Trait | In practice |
|---|---|
| Cool & editorial | Copy references culture with specificity. No generic adjectives. Names matter. |
| Bold | We take a point of view. "This is the one," not "this might be nice." |
| Luxury but approachable | Elevated language, never intimidating. A well-travelled friend who always knows the right place. |
| Indonesian at heart | We understand the audience's context — time zones, flight routes, the Jakarta–Bali dynamic. |

**Do:** write with confidence and cultural specificity ("Primavera Sound in May, when Barcelona is at its most electric"); mix short punchy sentences with longer evocative ones; name things with intention; trust the reader's intelligence; write in the second person.

**Don't:** use travel clichés ("unforgettable journey," "hidden gems"); be vague ("great experiences," "amazing lineup"); sound corporate; oversell; use excessive exclamation marks; write like a brochure.

**Tone by format:** social captions are punchy and atmospheric, often ending on an open note rather than a hard CTA. Package descriptions open with atmosphere, name 2–3 things DAYS provides, and close on a feeling, not a feature. Strategy docs are clear and confident, grounded in customer insight. Campaign concepts lead with one named big idea.

## Naming

DAYS packages are named like cultural events, not products — the way a festival names its stages or a label names its releases.

**Principles:** evocative, not descriptive; often alliterative, atmospheric or referential; should feel like something you'd put on a poster.

**Reference examples:** *Into The Wild: Borneo* · *Circuit Weekends* · *The Palma Edit* · *After Hours: Amsterdam*

## Package structure

Every DAYS package description moves through, even unlabelled:
1. **The hook** — why this moment, why now
2. **The experience** — what you actually do, see, feel
3. **The curation layer** — what DAYS adds that you couldn't do alone
4. **The feel** — how you'll leave feeling
